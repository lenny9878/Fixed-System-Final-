Blade gaps take 0.83 m² at the 0.5 cm spacing in use — worth about €1.75 of roll. Tightening the gap would reduce that, but only if your cutting method can hold the smaller spacing safely, which nobody has confirmed here. Settings
These apply to every calculation.

Margin is the accuracy allowance, 1 cm on every side. Leave extra seam at 0 unless a particular job needs more than that. Both are per side, so together they add twice to the width and twice to the height of every panel. Blade gap is the space left between panels, and any material can override it on its own record. Keep offcuts is the smallest strip worth saving — anything narrower is counted as scrap.

Blade-gap safety

Picking a cutting method fills in a typical starting blade gap. The tool will not price a tighter gap as a saving until the minimum safe gap is set and confirmed here. A gap too tight for the blade does not cost a strip, it costs a panel.

Layout templates
Data
Round-trips with BAAL-Material-Archive.xlsx. Column names have to match the workbook, so export once to see them.

Save the sheet as CSV in Excel, then choose it here. Which sheet it is is read from the headers. Materials match on Code and products on Item, so re-importing updates rather than duplicates. Role says which material a panel is cut from and Role material carries that material's code, so a lined product round-trips complete. Offcut rows replace that material's offcut list outright, because the file is the record.

Existing column names have not changed. Format, rotation rule, blade gap, roll lengths and panel rotation are appended after the old columns, so an older workbook still imports — those fields simply fall back to Roll, allow 90°, the workshop blade gap, and no recorded piece lengths.

Roll lengths cm holds the pieces you actually hold, longest first, separated by | — for example 300 | 220 | 145. Leave it blank where nobody has measured; the tool will say the length is unchecked rather than work one out from stock area.
